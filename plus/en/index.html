Privacy Policy for Star Chart Plus

In short

Star Chart Plus stores everything on the device: no servers, no account, no sign-in, no adverts, no analytics.

There is one difference to the free version, and it concerns the purchase only: Plus costs a one-off price, paid in the Play Store before the app is installed. What happens in the process is described under "The purchase" below.

Which data does the app process?

Stored on the device is only what parents enter themselves:

  • The children's first names and the chosen animal picture. A first name is enough; the app does not ask for a surname, a date of birth or a photo.
  • Tasks with their star value, their time and their weekly goal.
  • Completions — which task was ticked off on which day.
  • Rewards and when which child redeemed stars for them.
  • Badges a child has earned.
  • Optional parent accesses, each with their own code, and a log of who changed a star by hand. The codes are stored derived, not in clear text.

The app collects nothing by itself: no device identifier, no advertising ID, no location, no usage statistics.

The parent codes are not stored in clear text, only a value derived from them. Anyone inspecting the app's storage cannot read them from there. > They still are what they are: four digits that keep children out of the parent area. That is not a password — anyone who has the derived value and is willing to try ten thousand possibilities will get there. So do not use a code you use anywhere else.

The purchase

Plus is bought once. No subscription, no follow-up costs, no in-app purchases.

Google Play handles the purchase, not this app. Payment happens in the Play Store before the app reaches the device; the app itself contains no payment flow and knows nothing of the purchase — it runs because it is installed.

We see no payment data. No card number, no bank details, no billing address, no name, no email address. None of it reaches this app or its developer.

What Google processes as part of the purchase — Google account, payment method, purchase history — is governed by Google's terms and lies outside this app. The developer receives only settlement summaries from Google, without details about the buyer.

Where does the entered data go?

Nowhere — the app has no server, no user account and no synchronisation. The first names, stars, tasks and rewards do not leave the device; they are not part of the purchase either.

The Android backup is switched on. That means: if backup to Google is enabled on the device, Android takes this app's data along — so the collected stars survive a change of device. That is done by the operating system, not by the app; the data then sits encrypted in the user's Google account and is subject to their terms. Anyone who does not want this switches the backup off in the Android settings (usually under "System → Backup").

The backup to another phone

In the parent area the data can be handed to another phone — through the share sheet (Quick Share, for instance) or as a QR code on the screen that the other phone scans in its app. Either way the data goes directly from device to device; no server is involved. The QR code contains the complete data, compressed — whoever photographs it has the data. Show it only to the phone that is meant to receive it.

The backup you create yourself

In the parent area everything can be saved as a file. You decide where it goes: the device opens its share dialogue, and the file goes wherever you send it — into an email to yourself, into a cloud, into the device's storage.

The app itself sends nothing. It puts the file into its own cache and hands it to the dialogue; it needs no permission for that.

⚠️ What you pass on is complete. The file contains the children's first names, their stars, tasks and rewards — and the derived values of the parent codes. A backup in someone else's cloud sits under their terms, not ours.

The certificate

For every milestone in the star jar Plus issues a certificate. It is drawn on the device and handed to the share dialogue — where it goes is up to you. It carries the child's first name, its animal picture and the date. It does not come to us.

Analytics, adverts, sharing

The app contains no analytics tool, no crash reporting SDK and no adverts. No data is passed on to third parties or sold.

The fonts, too, are part of the program itself. The app loads nothing from external servers — in particular no fonts from Google Fonts, which would otherwise send the device's address on every start.

Children

The app is meant for families: parents create children, tasks and rewards, children tick things off and see how they are doing. Nothing is collected and nothing is transmitted in the process either. There is nothing to buy inside the app — neither for children nor for parents.

Permissions

The app requests one permission: the camera, and only when you scan a backup as a QR code from another phone in the parent area. It asks the first time you scan, not at start-up. The camera runs while the scan window is open and is switched off when it closes. No image is stored or sent: the app only looks for the code in the frames and discards them. If you refuse, you can send the backup through the share sheet instead.

Nothing else: not internet, not location, not microphone, not contacts, not storage — and no permission for purchases either, because there are none inside the app.

Deletion

All data sits in the app's storage on the device. It disappears completely when the app is uninstalled or its storage is cleared (Android settings → Apps → Star Chart Plus → Storage → Clear data). Nothing is left behind, because there is nothing anywhere else.

The purchase itself stays tied to your Google account; Plus can be installed again with it without paying twice. That is managed in the Play Store, not here.

If the Android backup was used, a copy of the entered data remains there. It is deleted through the user's Google account settings.
